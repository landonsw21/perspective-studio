# Perspective Studio

A browser-based perspective drafting tool, from 1-point perspective up to a full 360° 6-point view. Build boxes that snap to the grid, divide their faces in perspective and export the drawing as a PNG.

Live at https://landonsw21.github.io/perspective-studio/

## Perspective modes

Choose the mode from the perspective dropdown on the toolbar, which lists 4-point's fisheye and panorama as separate choices.

- **1-point:** one vanishing point on the horizon; depth edges run to it while widths stay horizontal and heights vertical, so box fronts are true rectangles. The VP drags anywhere, and the horizon line moves with it. Comes with a starburst of rays plus horizontal and vertical guides
- **2-point:** two vanishing points on the horizon, vertical edges stay vertical
- **3-point:** a third vanishing point above or below the horizon that vertical edges run to. A camera check in the panel says whether the setup matches a real camera. A setup that doesn't still works, but its boxes look stretched and carry their proportions to other modes only approximately
- **4-point:** two styles. *Fisheye* looks at a box's corner, like 2-point seen through a curved lens: the box's two horizontal directions vanish at VP1 and VP2 on the horizon, always 90° apart, and vertical lines curve to up and down VPs above and below. *Panorama* is a 360° strip with four vanishing points on the horizon, each horizontal direction vanishing at two points 180° apart, and straight verticals
- **5-point:** a fisheye looking straight ahead: depth lines run straight to a center vanishing point, lines across curve to the left and right VPs, and lines up and down curve to the up and down VPs
- **6-point:** the whole sphere in one continuous view: the front vanishing point at the center, left, right, up and down around the 90° ring, and the back vanishing point stretched around the outer rim, where every line heading behind you meets

## Curved views (4-, 5- and 6-point)

- **Horizontal and vertical curvature are set separately.** Horizontal curvature bends the lines that run left and right, and vertical curvature the lines that run up and down. 100% is an even fisheye, 0% makes those lines straight (straight verticals with curved horizontals, for example), and higher values bend them further. A panorama's vertical lines always stay straight, so it has horizontal curvature only
- **Every vanishing point moves on its own.** Drag a VP and only that one moves, while the lens reshapes on that side to match:
  - In 5- and 6-point, the left, right, up and down VPs slide along their lines, and the center slides between the left and right VPs. In 6-point, dragging the back rim grows or shrinks it, and the rim stays put while the other VPs move
  - In the 4-point fisheye, dragging VP1 or VP2 works like 2-point: the other one stays put while the lens turns and resizes, and the up and down VPs keep their places
  - In the panorama, each of the four VPs slides along the horizon on its own
  - **Even out VPs**, on the toolbar next to Fit view, puts them back in an even layout
- Lens size, the lens center, 5-point's coverage, the 6-point back rim and the 4-point turn are in the Perspective panel
- Every curved view projects true 3D lines through its camera, so boxes, snapping and face divisions work the same way everywhere. Setups saved with the earlier single curvature setting open with their lens rings exactly where they were

## Boxes

- Rectangular prisms that snap to ray intersections, guide lines and the corners of other boxes, shown solid, ghosted or as wireframes. A ghosted box's opacity slider runs from see-through to completely opaque, the faces behind included (they show where a face is hidden). Shift-click a face to hide it. Hold ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows and Linux) over a hidden face to see a ghost of it, and ⌘-click (Ctrl-click) to bring it back. Hidden faces come back furthest back first, a box's back face before the front face over it, even where faces you can see are in the way
- Set a box's width, length and height exactly under Size and place in the panel's Boxes section. Sizes are in pixels as the box looks where the middle of its base sits (focal length over depth), so dragging the box around in 1-, 2- and 3-point leaves them alone; in the curved views they're close but not exact. Width and length grow out from the middle of the base and height grows upward, so the box stays in place and its other two sizes don't change; equal sizes make a true cube. A size too big for the view stops at the largest one it can show
- **Center on horizon**, a checkbox for each box, keeps the horizon line running through the middle of the box's height, so neither its top nor its bottom shows. Ticking it moves the box straight up or down to get there, keeping its size and its place across the view. While it's on, dragging the box slides it along the horizon, its top handles and Height grow it evenly above and below, and moving the horizon or a VP or switching modes keeps it centered
- **Space the rays along this box**, a checkbox for each box (one box at a time), runs every VP's rays through the box's corners and division points at the same spacing as its subdivision lines, so the rays continue those lines, and on past the box (the ray count sets how many at each end). The vertical guides line up with the box the same way, and in 1-point so do the horizontal guides; their line counts set how many carry on past each end, and their spacing and offset step aside while the box sets them. Everything follows the box's front faces, or its back faces once the front ones are all hidden:
  - The vertical guides, and rays that run up and down (VP3, or the up and down VPs), stand at the vertical edges and division lines of the two front faces and carry on past each far end at the same spacing in perspective. Looking straight at one face, they follow just that face, past both ends. With both front faces hidden they move to the two back faces, running out from the back corner where those meet
  - Rays that run along the sides go through the vertical edge where those faces meet (the corner nearest you, or the back corner), so they continue the division lines on both. 1-point's horizontal guides run level with the division heights on that edge
  - Pattern letters count from the side of the corner nearest you, as the faces' division lines do. The rays and guides hold still while the box is being dragged, so it can snap to them, and catch up when it's let go
  - **Rays run along** Vertical faces, as above, or Top and bottom: a choice that appears once the box is ticked. Along the top and bottom, the rays below the horizon continue the division lines on the box's bottom, and the rays above it the ones on its top, at their spacing and on past the box, so the floor and ceiling grids carry on around it. A box below eye level gets only the bottom's (both faces' lines run below the horizon there), a box above eye level only the top's, and a box across eye level both, one on each side of the horizon, so lines never double up. The vertical guides, and VP3's or the up and down rays, then stand where those lines meet the edges of the faces in use. In 1-point the horizontal guides follow the same split: the bottom's lines below the horizon, the top's above it. Setups saved before open with Vertical faces
  - In 6-point and the panorama, where ordinary rays run all the way around the sphere, the rays spaced along a box are just the box's own lines: each runs from its VP, through the box, round to the opposite VP. The rest of its circle would be the same line mirrored through your eye, behind you and on the other side of the horizon, so it's left out and the lines don't double up there. For the same reason, 5-point's Extend behind the VP steps aside while a box spaces the rays
- Drag a base corner to reshape the base, or a top corner to change the height. A base corner can't be dragged across the horizon, where the box's far edges would shoot back out of the vanishing points
- Drag a box's body to move it. The move is rigid: the box keeps its real proportions wherever it goes, in every mode. Dragged across eye level, it hops over the horizon rather than flattening onto it
- In 1-, 2- and 3-point, an edit stops before a box reaches so far to the side that it would stretch out toward infinity
- Perspective-correct face divisions (1/2, 1/4, 1/8 …) and diagonals: an X corner to corner, in every column, in every row or in every cell, or a single diagonal across every cell, either way round. On the upright faces, columns stand side by side and rows are stacked up them. On the top and bottom, columns sit side by side along the edges running to VP1, and rows along those running to VP2
- Division lines have a pattern and style table like the rays. Each letter from A to Z is a line style with its own color, weight, line and opacity, a dot skips a line, and the pattern repeats. Letters count from the box's corner nearest you, along each edge and up from the base, the same way as for rays spaced along a box, so a pattern lines up all the way around. A's style also draws the diagonals, and "Thinner lines at each finer split" still thins each finer level. Setups saved before keep their one color, weight and opacity as A's, so they look the same

## Boxes keep their place, size and proportions

- **Switching perspective** keeps every box at the same place and size on screen, with the same real proportions. Each box is read as a 3D shape through the current mode's camera and rebuilt in the new one, so nothing needs re-zooming or re-placing after a switch
- **Switching into 4-, 5- or 6-point** grows the lens or panorama (it never shrinks on its own) until every box fits at its old size and place. In 6-point it also keeps boxes that were in front of you in front of you, rather than wrapped around toward the rim
- **Moving the horizon or a vanishing point** in 1-, 2- or 3-point rebuilds every box on the new grid at the same place and size, with the same proportions
- **In 4-, 5- and 6-point**, moving the horizon, dragging a lens VP or changing the curvature, lens size, center, coverage, back rim or turn keeps boxes where they stand in the scene, so they move with the view
- Where a view can't show a box that large or that far out, the box gets as close as it can, and switching back restores it exactly

## Everything else

- Editable horizon line: eye level, center, tilt, color, weight, line style
- Draggable vanishing points whose markers take the color of their primary rays, with an optional cone of vision
- Ray fans from each vanishing point: even spacing or even angles, any count, and a repeating pattern of letters. Each letter from A to Z is a ray style with its own color, line, weight and opacity. The line is solid, dashed, dotted, dash-dot, long dash or dash-dot-dot (the horizon and guides offer the same six). Weight and opacity are sliders that show their value when you hover over them. The weight slider runs from 0 to 8 px, and letting go of it at its end doubles its range, up to 500 px. A dot skips a ray, and a letter gets a row in the style table as soon as it's typed. VP1 and VP2 can be edited together, sharing everything but their colors
- A sized export area that renders to PNG at up to 8× (capped at 64 megapixels)
- **Scale the drawing** to fit the export area. Drag the round handle just off the export area's bottom-right corner in toward the middle to shrink everything (the horizon, vanishing points, rays, guides and boxes), or out to enlarge it. The scale goes in whole percent, shown beside the handle as you drag, and everything scales around the middle of the export area. The export area itself and line weights stay as they are. Smaller and Larger buttons under Export area step by 10%. Every mode scales together, so switching perspective afterwards gives the same boxes as scaling after the switch. A lens or panorama stops at the smallest or largest size it can take. Scaling works with the horizon and VPs locked too, since everything moves together
- Number boxes accept values past the end of each slider, and the slider stretches to fit; releasing a slider at its end doubles its range
- Undo from the toolbar or with Ctrl/⌘ Z; redo with Ctrl/⌘ Shift Z or Ctrl/⌘ Y. The shortcuts also work right after you use a slider, checkbox, color picker or dropdown. Only while you're typing in a number or text box are they left to that box
- Save a setup as a JSON file and load it back later. A file that isn't a setup is refused. In one that is, values that don't make sense fall back to the defaults, and boxes with unusable corners are left out

Everything runs in a single `index.html` with no build step. Work is saved in the visitor's own browser.

## Testing

Stress tested from 27 to 29 October 2026 with scripted runs in the browser, covering all six modes and both 4-point styles:

- **The curved-view lens:** 14,023 directions sent to the screen and back under random settings, including 0% and very high curvature and uneven sides, came back to within 0.000002°. At 0% vertical curvature every vertical box edge in the 4-point fisheye, 5- and 6-point is straight to the pixel (0.0000 px off), and so is every horizontal edge in 5-point at 0% horizontal curvature and every edge of a box in front of you in a panorama at 0%; at 100% they bend by 3–31 px. Four setups saved with the old single curvature setting reopened with their 90° rings and 6-point rims exactly where they were
- **Moving VPs one at a time:** 475 random drags of single lens VPs, across all four curved views. Each moved only that VP, landed under the pointer (or at its limit, such as a 6-point ring point meeting the back rim), left every box's proportions alone, and undid and redid exactly
- **Switching perspective:** 225 random switches on nine scenes of 20 random boxes of different sizes, shapes and positions. Every box kept its proportions exactly, its size to within 0.5% and its center to within 1.6% of its size
- **Dragging:** 2,468 random drags on 84 scenes of 8 boxes, covering base corners, tops, bodies, the horizon, vanishing points and lens VPs. A moved box always kept its proportions and the other boxes stayed exactly where they were. Moving the horizon or a vanishing point kept every box's proportions, and in 1-, 2- and 3-point its place and size too. No base ended up lying on or straddling the horizon. Every drag that changed anything was undone and redone, and both restored the drawing exactly. In 1-point, 40 more drags took the VP up to 400 px in any direction on level and tilted horizons: each time the VP landed under the pointer with the horizon running through it, and every box kept its place, size and proportions
- **Panel values:** 2,070 random edits to the horizon, vanishing points and lens settings, 1,160 of them at extreme values or on a randomly reshaped lens. No box changed proportions, every box in 1-, 2- and 3-point kept its place and size, and every edit undid and redid exactly
- **Ray patterns:** in all six modes and both 4-point styles, every letter of a pattern was drawn in its own color, weight, line and opacity, with the A rays on top, in the view and in the exported PNG. Dots skipped exactly the rays they should, setups saved with the earlier A/B styles opened looking the same, and typing a pattern or changing a letter's style undid and redid exactly
- **Ray weight sliders:** weights were set by dragging with the mouse, by the arrow keys (0.05 px a press) and directly. With VP1 and VP2 edited together, a weight changed on both; otherwise only on the VP being edited. The rays were drawn at that weight in the view and in the exported PNG. Each release was a single undo step, and undo and redo brought back the weights and the sliders exactly. Letting go at the end doubled the range from 8 px to 16, 32, 64, 128, 256 and 500 px, and no further. A 40 px weight in a saved setup opened with its slider running to 64 px, and a letter added to the pattern got its own sliders. On a 375 px wide phone screen nothing scrolls sideways, and "Dash-dot-dot" still fits its dropdown
- **Division patterns:**
  - **Older setups:** 28 random scenes of up to 8 boxes, covering all six modes and both 4-point styles, were saved with the earlier single color, weight and opacity. Each exported exactly the same PNG, pixel for pixel, as the previous version, and all 214 boxes carried their settings over as style A.
  - **Letters on every line:** 34 random scenes of up to 5 boxes had random patterns (dots and uneven ones included) and random division counts, in both solid and ghosted display, with the finer-split thinning on and off. All 9,780 division lines were drawn in the letter you get by counting from the box's nearest corner in 3D, at the right weight, line and opacity, and dots skipped exactly their lines.
  - **Controls:** presets, typed patterns, the style table, copying to every box, duplicating, undo, redo, and saving and loading all worked. A saved pattern with junk in it fell back cleanly.
  - **Speed and regressions:** redrawing 34 boxes with divisions and diagonals took the same time as the previous version measured side by side. The drag, switch and panel tests passed again (175, 30 and 140 operations).
- **Scaling the drawing:**
  - **Exactness:** 42 random scenes in all six modes and both 4-point styles were scaled from 5% to 600%, with rays at even spacing and at even angles, rays spaced along a box and random lens settings. Every VP, the horizon, every box corner and edge, ray, guide, ray crossing and lens rim landed where scaling around the middle of the export area puts it: boxes to within 0.00000000001 px, guides to within 0.00004 px. Every box kept its proportions, its sizes in the panel scaled by the same amount, and boxes centered on the horizon stayed centered.
  - **The handle and buttons:** 35 drags of the handle (31% to 148%) each scaled by the percentage shown, and each undid and redid exactly in one step. A click, or a drag back to where it started, changed nothing. Smaller followed by Larger came back exactly, and both stopped where a lens would get too small or too large. A scaled setup saved and loaded exactly.
  - **Switching perspective:** scaling then switching matched switching then scaling in 27 random pairs, to within 1.6% of each box's size, the same accuracy as a switch on its own.
  - **Older setups and speed:** setups saved before open at their original scale and draw exactly as they did. 35 scenes matched pixel for pixel, including even-angle fans in the curved views and 1-point with its VPs close in. A step of a scale drag takes about 2 ms in 1-, 2- and 3-point and 8–15 ms in the curved views.
- **Diagonals:** in all six modes and both 4-point styles, every diagonal option drew exactly the lines it should on each of 4,704 faces (80,192 diagonal lines) and nothing else, at A's weight (three-quarters of it in cells). That includes the new column, row and flipped-cell options. The four earlier options draw exactly as before, pixel for pixel, in 28 scenes. The random drag, switch and panel tests passed again afterwards (184, 30 and 140 operations, no failures)
- **Undo shortcuts:** ⌘ Z and ⌘ Shift Z undid and redid the drawing when a slider, checkbox, color picker, dropdown or button had focus. In a number or text box they were left to the box. The random drag, switch and panel tests passed again afterwards (175, 30 and 140 operations, no failures)

Ticking a second box moved the rays to it, unticking brought back the Rays settings exactly, the rays held still during a drag of the box and ran exactly through its new corner afterwards, and the random drag tests passed in every mode with a box spacing the rays
- **Guides spaced along a box:**
  - **Placement:** 350 random scenes across all six modes and both 4-point styles each had a box spacing the rays, with random division counts and line counts: 182 with two front faces, 129 with both of them hidden, 26 looking straight at one face and 13 with that face hidden. The guides held still while the box was dragged and followed it afterwards. Turning the option off, then undoing, restored everything exactly.
- **Rays along the top and bottom:**
  - **Placement:** in 182 random scenes across all six modes and both 4-point styles, a box's rays were sent along its top and bottom with the box below eye level (57), above it (48) and across it (77), and its top or bottom hidden now and then. Judged from the screen, every ray running down from its VP followed a division line on the bottom and every ray running up one on the top, with at most one set on each side of the horizon. The drag, switch and panel tests passed again (121, 30 and 140 operations), as did 173 random drags with a box spacing the rays along its top and bottom, each undone and redone exactly.
- **Rays spaced along a box in 6-point and the panorama:**
  - **Placement:** 84 random scenes in 6-point, the panorama and 5-point had a box spacing the rays along its vertical faces or its top and bottom, with the box below eye level, above it or across it, and 5-point's Extend behind the VP ticked in 17 of its 28 scenes.
