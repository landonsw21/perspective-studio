# Perspective Studio

A browser-based perspective drafting tool, from 1-point perspective up to a full 360° 6-point view. Build boxes that snap to the grid, divide their faces in perspective and export the drawing as a PNG, JPG or PDF.

Live at https://landonsw21.github.io/perspective-studio/

## Perspective modes

Choose the mode from the perspective dropdown on the toolbar, which lists 4-point's fisheye and panorama as separate choices.

- **1-point:** one vanishing point on the horizon; depth edges run to it while widths stay horizontal and heights vertical, so box fronts are true rectangles. The VP drags anywhere, and the horizon line moves with it. Comes with a starburst of rays plus horizontal and vertical guides
- **2-point:** two vanishing points on the horizon, vertical edges stay vertical
- **3-point:** a third vanishing point above or below the horizon that vertical edges run to. A camera check, behind its own **ⓘ Camera check** button in the Camera section, says whether the setup matches a real camera. **Even out VPs**, on the toolbar, spaces all three evenly: VP1 and VP2 go either side of the center point, as far apart as they were, and VP3 goes straight below it, or above if that's where it was, all three the same distance apart. The view then looks down (or up) along a cube's diagonal, so every direction shrinks alike, and it always matches a real camera. It also centers the layout on the page, as **Center horizon on page** does: the horizon's center point goes to the middle of the export area. Boxes keep their place, size and proportions. A setup that doesn't still works, but its boxes look stretched and carry their proportions to other modes only approximately
- **Isometric:** no vanishing points and no horizon. Box edges stay parallel, at 30° either side of level and straight up and down, and nothing shrinks with distance, so a box's sizes are its edge lengths on the page wherever it sits. The rays become three sets of parallel lines with one spacing and count between them, making a grid of equal triangles (each set has its own offset, pattern and styles). Boxes, snapping, face divisions, rays spaced along a box and export all work as in the other modes. Along the top and bottom, rays follow the box's bottom, since everything sits below eye level. Center on horizon doesn't apply here: a box keeps the setting and centers again when you switch back
- **4-point:** two styles. *Fisheye* looks at a box's corner, like 2-point seen through a curved lens: the box's two horizontal directions vanish at VP1 and VP2 on the horizon, always 90° apart, and vertical lines curve to up and down VPs above and below. *Panorama* is a 360° strip with four vanishing points on the horizon, each horizontal direction vanishing at two points 180° apart, and straight verticals
- **5-point:** a fisheye looking straight ahead: depth lines run straight to a center vanishing point, lines across curve to the left and right VPs, and lines up and down curve to the up and down VPs
- **6-point:** the whole sphere in one continuous view: the front vanishing point at the center, left, right, up and down around the 90° ring, and the back vanishing point stretched around the outer rim, where every line heading behind you meets, as if the camera sat inside a globe. Nothing stops at the back: drag a box past the rim and it goes through the point behind you and comes in from the opposite side

**Reset**, on the toolbar beside the perspective dropdown, puts the view in use back as it starts: its horizon, vanishing points and lens at their defaults, the horizon placed on the page where a new drawing has it, and the boxes replaced by the one box a new drawing starts with, in the middle of the page and sized so its outline takes up about a quarter of it. Vanishing points that would land on the page are pushed out past it, by growing the lens or moving the VPs further out, until each is clear of it by a tenth of its height. The one straight ahead, 1-point's VP, 5-point's center or 6-point's front, is the middle of the view and stays there. A scaled drawing first goes back to 100%, so ray and guide spacing and the other views' lenses are as they were before it was scaled. Colors, rays, guides, the page and the other views' lens settings stay as they are. Its tooltip and the message it shows name the view (Reset 2-point, Reset 4-point fisheye and so on), and one undo (⌘ Z or Ctrl Z) brings back everything it replaced.

## Curved views (4-, 5- and 6-point)

- **Horizontal and vertical curvature are set separately.** Horizontal curvature bends the lines that run left and right, and vertical curvature the lines that run up and down. 100% is an even fisheye, 0% makes those lines straight (straight verticals with curved horizontals, for example), and higher values bend them further. A panorama's vertical lines always stay straight, so it has horizontal curvature only
- **Every vanishing point moves on its own**, unless Mirror is on (below). Drag a VP and only that one moves, while the lens reshapes on that side to match:
  - In 5- and 6-point, the left, right, up and down VPs slide along their lines, and the center slides between the left and right VPs. In 6-point, dragging the back rim grows or shrinks it, and the rim stays put while the other VPs move
  - In the 4-point fisheye, dragging VP1 or VP2 works like 2-point: the other one stays put while the lens turns and resizes, and the up and down VPs keep their places
  - In the panorama, each of the four VPs slides along the horizon on its own
  - **Mirror VPs around the center**, at the top of the Vanishing points section (the same setting as 2- and 3-point's Mirror), moves each VP's partner the same distance the other way as you drag: VP1 and VP2 in the fisheye, VP1 and VP2 and also VP3 and VP4 in the panorama, and the left and right points and the up and down points in the fisheye, 5- and 6-point. A lopsided lens evens out the moment you drag one of a pair. With Mirror on, dragging 5-point's center or 6-point's front VP moves the whole lens, so its sides stay even. 6-point's back rim stays put as the sides move, as it does without Mirror
  - **Even out VPs**, on the toolbar next to Fit view, puts them back in an even layout and centers it on the page: the lens center goes to the middle of the export area, with the boxes riding along as they do for any horizon move
- Lens size, the lens center, 5-point's coverage and the 4-point turn are under **Lens** (or **Panorama**) in the Camera section; the 6-point back rim, which is where the back VP lies, is in the Vanishing points section's Front & back tab
- Every curved view projects true 3D lines through its camera, so boxes, snapping and face divisions work the same way everywhere. Setups saved with the earlier single curvature setting open with their lens rings exactly where they were

## Boxes

- Rectangular prisms that snap to ray intersections, guide lines and the corners of other boxes, every one drawn ghosted at its **Opacity**, from 0% (see-through, as a wireframe was) to 100% (solid, as a solid box was), the faces behind included (they show where a face is hidden); new boxes start ghosted at 30%, and boxes saved solid or as wireframes open at 100% or 0%. **Click a face or a subdividing plane** of any box (Alt, or hiding the faces in front, reaches one behind) to edit it on its own in a pop-up beside the click. A click opens it; a drag only moves the box, and a drag straight after the click (a tap and then a drag on a trackpad) puts it away again. The pop-up holds:
  - **Show**, to hide the face or bring it back, and the box's turn around the axis through it (the same angle as its slider and its rotation circle)
  - its **Opacity** and **Color**
  - its **Edges'** color, weight and opacity. An edge it shares with a face that has no edges of its own takes its look; between two that both have, the one in front wins
  - its **Divisions**: on or off, and how many lines run each way, named for where they run (to VP1, to VP2, vertical, and so on in each view), which the rays spaced along the box follow too
  - its division lines' color, weight and opacity, standing in for every letter of the pattern
  - its **Diagonals**, with their own color, weight and opacity (A's, after the face's line look, until set)
  - **Use the box's settings**, which drops everything it was given of its own so it follows the box again

  Each setting starts as the box's and becomes the face's own once changed. With one picked, **Shift-click** more, on any box, to edit them together (Shift-click one again to drop it); with none picked, Shift-click still hides a face. The ones picked are outlined while it's up. Drag it by its title to move it; ×, Escape or a click on empty space puts it away. A plane's own settings go when its direction's count changes, as its hidden state does, and opacities set with the O key in earlier versions open as the faces' own. Drag a box by a face and the corner you can see nearest where you took hold, top or bottom, is the one that snaps to intersections as it moves (with a face toward you see-through or hidden, any corner, since you see them all). Hold Shift over a face to see it ghosted in its own color, as it will look once hidden, with the faces behind showing through, and Shift-click to hide it. Hold ⌘ (Ctrl on Windows and Linux) over a hidden face to see a ghost of it, and ⌘-click (Ctrl-click) to bring it back. Hidden faces come back furthest back first, a box's back face before the front face over it, even where faces you can see are in the way. Faces are hidden and brought back on the canvas only; under Faces in the Boxes section, **Show all faces** brings every hidden face back and **Open top** hides the top, each greyed out when there's nothing for it to do
- **Show hidden edges as dashes**, a checkbox for each box, dashes an edge only where something of the box you can see is in front of it: a face, or a subdividing plane. The rest of it is drawn solid, like the front edges, so with a face hidden the edges and planes you look at through the opening are solid, and where a plane meets a face you can see the line is solid too. It works the same at any opacity: hidden edges are dashed where you see them through the faces, and as a box (or a face in front) nears opaque, dashed faintly over it too
- **Subdividing planes** cut a box into equal slices across its width (X), its length (Y) and up its height (Z), set for each box under Planes in the Boxes section: halves, thirds, quarters and so on up to tenths, each way on its own. The number beside each choice counts the planes, so it reads 0 for none, 1 for halves and 2 for thirds, and typing a count picks the slices. The slices are measured in depth, like the face divisions, and the planes turn with the box and curve through the lenses. They're drawn the way the box's faces are: in its face color, shaded by which way they face (one across the height looks like the top from above and the bottom from below), see-through at the box's opacity (or a plane's own), as outlines at 0%, and out of sight inside a box at 100% until the faces in front of them are hidden or made see-through. Where they cross they're cut and drawn back to front, so each covers what's behind it, and their edges are dashed where something of the box is in front of them, like the box's own hidden edges. They carry the face divisions too: each plane draws the division lines of the faces it lies parallel to, with the same counts, pattern, line weights and diagonals, so the lines run on from the faces through the box. Divide the four sides covers the planes across X and Y, Divide top and bottom the ones across Z, and Divide the planes, under Face divisions once a box has planes, turns them all off. Shift-click a plane to hide it and ⌘-click (Ctrl-click) to bring it back, as with a face; **Show all planes** brings back every one, and changing a direction's count brings back that direction's. The pointer always reaches the nearest face or plane you can see, so a plane inside only once whatever is in front of it is hidden, and ⌘ brings a hidden plane back only from where nothing in front of it shows. Hovering a plane ghosts it in its axis color with its rotation circle, and clicking it opens its own pop-up, as a face does
- Set a box's width, length and height exactly under Size and place in the panel's Boxes section. Sizes are in pixels as the box looks where the middle of its base sits (focal length over depth), so dragging the box around in 1-, 2- and 3-point leaves them alone; in the curved views they're close but not exact. Width and length grow out from the middle of the base and height grows upward, so the box stays in place and its other two sizes don't change; equal sizes make a true cube. A size too big for the view stops at the largest one it can show
- **Center on horizon**, a checkbox for each box, keeps the horizon line running through the middle of the box's height, so neither its top nor its bottom shows. Ticking it moves the box straight up or down to get there, keeping its size and its place across the view. While it's on, dragging the box slides it along the horizon, its top handles and Height grow it evenly above and below, and moving the horizon or a VP or switching modes keeps it centered
- **Space the rays along this box**, a checkbox for each box (one box at a time), runs the rays through the edges and division lines of the faces and planes of the box you tick, at the same spacing as its subdivision lines, so the rays continue those lines
  - **Rays snap to** is a dropdown of checkboxes: closed, it names what's ticked (Near side, to VP1 + Top, say); open, it lists the box's faces, named for where they sit (Near side, to VP1; Far side, to VP2; Top; Bottom, and so on), and, once the box has subdividing planes, its planes (X plane 1, Z plane 2…). A press anywhere else closes it. Turning on Space the rays along this box ticks the front face you look at most squarely, and the last box ticked can't be unticked
  - Each VP's rays go through the edges and division lines of what's ticked that run to it, and the vertical guides (or VP3's, or the up and down VPs' rays) through the upright ones, or for a top, bottom or plane across the height, which has none, where its edges and division lines meet its two edges nearest you, so the verticals snap to the box whatever is ticked; 1-point's horizontal guides take the level lines across the picture. A face's division lines are its own; a plane's are those of the face it lies parallel to, when Divide the planes is on. Without divisions, just the edges. **With one ticked**, the rays carry on past it to the sides at the same spacing in perspective, as many at each end as each VP's count says (Rays past the box at each end); **with more**, they cross just those faces and planes and none run outside the box (the count past the box is hidden then)
  - A VP with no lines on what's ticked (VP2, say, with only a face running to VP1) keeps its own spacing and settings, as do 1-point's horizontal guides with nothing level across the picture ticked (the verticals always have something)
  - Pattern letters count from each face's or plane's corner nearest you, as its division lines do. The rays and guides hold still while the box is being dragged, so it can snap to them, and catch up when it's let go. A box saved before there was a choice follows the front face it looks at most squarely, or along the top and bottom the top or bottom you can see
  - In 6-point and the panorama, where ordinary rays run all the way around the sphere, the rays spaced along a box are just the box's own lines: each runs from its VP, through the box, round to the opposite VP. The rest of its circle would be the same line mirrored through your eye, behind you and on the other side of the horizon, so it's left out and the lines don't double up there. For the same reason, Extend behind the VP steps aside in every view while a box spaces the rays, so each of its rays stops at its VP. Carried on past it, in 1-, 2-, 3- or 5-point, the rays would come back across the horizon as those mirrored lines, doubling the grid along the top and bottom. The setting comes back when the box is unticked
- Drag a base corner to reshape the base, or a top corner to change the height. A base corner can't be dragged across the horizon, where the box's far edges would shoot back out of the vanishing points
- Drag a box's body to move it. The move is rigid: the box keeps its real proportions wherever it goes, in every mode. Wherever you take hold of it, that spot stays under the pointer: in 1-, 2- and 3-point and isometric the box slides across at the same depth, so it keeps its size, and in the curved views it keeps its distance from you. Dragged across eye level, it hops over the horizon rather than flattening onto it. In the fisheye and 5-point a rotated box goes past the edge of the lens just as one that isn't rotated does
- **Drag a box past either end of the panorama** and it comes round from the other end, rotated or not. A box lying across the two ends can be held by the piece at either end and dragged either way
- **In 6-point, drag a box past the rim** and it carries on through the point behind you, coming in from the opposite side, rotated or not. The whole rim is that one point, so a box near it stretches along the rim, and one right over it wraps the whole rim as a ring
- **Copy, cut and paste** a box with ⌘ C, ⌘ X and ⌘ V (Ctrl C, X and V on Windows and Linux). A paste goes under the pointer, moved there by its middle the way dragging the box would take it, so it keeps its depth and size, or gets as near as the view allows. With the pointer still where it was when you copied or last pasted, or off the drawing, it goes beside the box instead: along the horizon at the same depth, clear of the box and of any other within five widths, on whichever side is nearer (the right when they're about the same). Pasting again puts the next copy beside that one. A copy gets a new name and leaves the rays with the original. The first paste after a cut moves the box rather than copying it: it goes back where it was, or under the pointer, keeping its name and its rays unless another box has taken them since. Pasted after switching perspective or moving the horizon, a VP or the lens, a copy is rebuilt with the same proportions at the place and size on screen it had, as a switch rebuilds every box. In a number or text box, or with text selected on the page, the keys copy and paste as usual. **Duplicate**, in the Boxes section, places its copy beside the box the same way; in 1-, 2- and 3-point and isometric it used to slide the copy across the page, which changed its proportions
- **Which box is in front** goes by distance unless you say otherwise:
  - **By distance**, the default: the nearer box goes in front, in the view, in exports and for clicks. A drawing doesn't say how far off a box is, so boxes share one ground below eye level, and the lower on the page, the nearer. A level box whose base sits inside another's top stands on that box, so stacks hold together, and a box wholly above eye level hangs the same height above it. Each overlapping pair is worked out from the boxes themselves, not their middles, so a small box in front of the far end of a long wall stays in front of it. Boxes that pass through each other have no right order. They go by whichever is nearer along most of the lines of sight that meet both, and no line is drawn where they cross
  - **Custom order:** the list in the Boxes section runs from the box in front to the one at the back. Drag a box in it to put it in front of or behind another, or press Alt (⌥) and the left or right arrow on a focused box, and Box order switches to Custom order, where new boxes go in front. Box order switches back and forth and keeps your custom order. Setups saved earlier open by distance, with the order they were drawn in kept as the custom one
- **Rotate boxes** around their three axes, in every mode:
  - **Rotation circles:** hover over a face of any box, selected or not, to highlight that face in its axis's color and show its circle, ghosted too: it goes around the box, square to the axis through that face, like Rhino's rotation handles, red for X, green for Y, blue for Z. The circle stays while you head for it, across empty space and even across other faces (the highlight moves to another face only once the pointer stops on it), so you can go on to it and drag it straight away, which selects the box. On the selected box, faces you've hidden work the same way, picked from the empty space where they'd be. The nearest face or plane under the pointer that you can see comes first (where everything under it is hidden, the nearest hidden face), and holding Alt (⌥) reaches the one behind it. Holding Shift swaps the colored face and its circle for a plain ghost of the face you can see, and Shift-click hides it. Click the face and its circle turns solid, as the face's pop-up opens: drag it to turn the box around that axis, or click it to set that axis's angle from the box's default, the same angle its slider sets. While you drag, the angle beside the pointer is that one too, from 0 to 360, not how far this drag has turned it. The box opens with the current angle filled in and highlighted, so typing replaces it. Enter sets it, and Escape or clicking elsewhere leaves it. Four small arrows on the circle show which way is positive. Hovering over another face ghosts its circle alongside, and clicking that face moves the solid circle there. Clicking anywhere else puts it away, and dragging a face, hidden or not, still moves the box. On a touch screen, with no hovering, tap a face to make its circle solid. They're on by default; Rotation circles on faces, under Rotation in the Boxes section, turns them off (a face still highlights and opens its pop-up)
  - **Three sliders with number boxes** set the angles around X (width), Y (length) and Z (height), from 0° to 360°. Angles go round rather than building up: past 360 they carry on from 0, and below 0 they count back down from 360, whether you're spinning a circle, typing or stepping a number box, so -30 reads 330. Setups saved with negative angles open with the same rotation in this range. The box turns around X, then Y, then Z, about its middle, so Z alone keeps it level. Clear rotation squares it up again. An angle the view can't show is refused with a message
  - **Its own vanishing points** appear once a box is rotated, in the same colors and labeled with its name. Show this box's vanishing points hides them, and exports draw them when Include VP markers is on. Drag one to turn the box: it turns about its middle, the least way that puts that axis's edges on the point, and its other points move to stay square to them. The point catches on the horizon within 10 px, which keeps a level box level; hold Alt (⌥) or turn snapping off to stop that. A turn the view can't show is refused, as it is for the sliders. An axis still on one of the page's directions gets none, and neither does one that's too square to the view to meet on the page, so turning around Z alone gives two, on the horizon. Isometric has none. Curved views show one at each end of an axis where the lens reaches
  - **Its own rays** run back from the edges and division lines of a rotated box's front faces to its own vanishing points, in the same red, green and blue. Hidden faces send none, and an edge two front faces share sends one. Flat views draw each ray from the far end of its line to the vanishing point, or to the edge of the view when the point is further away than that. Curved views follow the lens to the points at both ends of the axis, where it reaches them. An axis too square to a flat view to meet on the page has no rays, and isometric's run parallel, since it has no points. They sit under the boxes, so solid faces cover them, and they aren't snap targets. Show rays from its front faces to them, under the vanishing point switch in the box's menu, turns them off, and exports draw them when Include rays is on. They're separate from the rays you space along a box, which stay on the page's VPs
  - **Everything else carries on:** handles reshape a rotated box along its own axes, sizes, snapping to its corners, face divisions, moves and export all work, and it keeps its angles through perspective switches and scaling. Rays spaced along a rotated box stay on the page's VPs, spaced as if it weren't rotated
- In 1-, 2- and 3-point, an edit stops before a box reaches so far to the side that it would stretch out toward infinity. Dragging a box can take it a little further, so a box made as big as editing allows can still be dragged every way. Where a drag can't follow the pointer (at that limit, or with the base on eye level), the box goes as far as it can and slides along the limit instead of stopping. A Width, Length or Height too big for the view stops at the largest it can show, never with the top behind you
- Perspective-correct face divisions (1/2, 1/4, 1/8 …) and diagonals: an X corner to corner, in every column, in every row or in every cell, or a single diagonal across every cell, either way round. On the upright faces, columns stand side by side and rows are stacked up them. On the top and bottom, columns sit side by side along the edges running to VP1, and rows along those running to VP2
- Division lines have a pattern and style table like the rays. Each letter from A to Z is a line style with its own color, weight, line and opacity, a dot skips a line, and the pattern repeats. Letters count from the box's corner nearest you, along each edge and up from the base, the same way as for rays spaced along a box, so a pattern lines up all the way around. A's style also draws the diagonals, and "Thinner lines at each finer split" still thins each finer level. Setups saved before keep their one color, weight and opacity as A's, so they look the same

## Boxes keep their place, size and proportions

- **Switching perspective** keeps every box at the same place and size on screen, with the same real proportions. Each box is read as a 3D shape through the current mode's camera and rebuilt in the new one, so nothing needs re-zooming or re-placing after a switch
- **Switching into 4-, 5- or 6-point** grows the lens or panorama (it never shrinks on its own) until every box fits at its old size and place. In 6-point it also keeps boxes that were in front of you in front of you, rather than wrapped around toward the rim
- **Moving the horizon or a vanishing point** in 1-, 2- or 3-point rebuilds every box on the new grid at the same place and size, with the same proportions
- **In 4-, 5- and 6-point**, moving the horizon, dragging a lens VP or changing the curvature, lens size, center, coverage, back rim or turn keeps boxes where they stand in the scene, so they move with the view
- Where a view can't show a box that large or that far out, the box gets as close as it can, and switching back restores it exactly

## Everything else

- A horizon line: drag the line to raise or lower eye level, or the diamond at its center to move it anywhere (where a vanishing point sits on the diamond, as in 1-point and the panorama to begin with, hold Alt to grab the diamond). **Horizon tilt** and **Center horizon on page**, at the top of the Vanishing points section, are its only settings: the line is always shown, in one color, weight and line style (to leave it out of an export, untick Horizon under Include in the Export area section), and setups saved with a hidden or restyled horizon open with the standard one. Center horizon on page puts its center point in the middle of the export area (in isometric, **Center grid on page** puts the middle of the grid there). Boxes keep their proportions, and in 1-, 2- and 3-point their place and size too, as with any horizon move; in the curved views they ride along with the lens
- Draggable vanishing points whose markers take the color of their primary rays, with an optional cone of vision (set under **Cone of vision** in the Camera section). The markers are always shown, all at one size; to leave them out of an export, untick **VP markers** under Include in the Export area section. In 2- and 3-point, VP1 and VP2 are placed by dragging their markers along the horizon or with **VP1 distance from center** and **VP2 distance from center** at the top of their own tabs in the Vanishing points section, each a slider with a box to type the distance in; **Mirror VPs around the center point** keeps the other one opposite either way (the curved views have it too; see Curved views)
- Ray fans from each vanishing point: even spacing or even angles, any count, and a repeating pattern of letters. Each letter from A to Z is a ray style with its own color, line, weight and opacity. The line is solid, dashed, dotted, dash-dot, long dash or dash-dot-dot (the horizon and guides offer the same six). Weight and opacity are sliders that show their value when you hover over them. The weight slider runs from 0 to 8 px, and letting go of it at its end doubles its range, up to 500 px. A dot skips a ray, and a letter gets a row in the style table as soon as it's typed. VP1 and VP2 can be edited together, sharing everything but their colors
- The panel opens with two sections for the picture's setup. **Camera** holds what belongs to the view as a whole: the view's notes and 3-point's camera check, the **Lens** or **Panorama** settings in the curved views and the **Cone of vision**. **Vanishing points** (called **Rays** in isometric, which has none) starts with Horizon tilt and Center horizon on page, then Mirror VPs around the center point and Lock horizon and VPs in place, then has a tab for each VP. A VP's tab starts with where that VP sits, when it has a setting of its own (VP1's or VP2's distance from center in 2- and 3-point, 1-point's VP position, VP3's position, its distance from the horizon and Look down or Look up in 3-point, and the back rim in 6-point's Front & back), then its rays under **Rays**. The tabs pick what to edit: a VP's rays, or, in 1-point, 2-point and the panorama, **Verticals** for the vertical guides, with a swatch in A's color. The Verticals tab is laid out like a VP's: Show, the count, spacing and offset, then the same pattern and style table as the rays, so guides can alternate styles, say a heavier line every fourth one. The pattern counts out from the middle guide or in from the outermost one, and along a box from the corner nearest you, and a dot skips a guide. Setups saved before keep their guides' one style as A's. 3-point and the other curved views have no separate guides: their vertical lines are the VP3, Up & down or vertical-axis rays. 1-point's horizontal guides keep their own section
- A sized export area that saves as PNG or JPG at up to 8× (capped at 64 megapixels), or as a vector PDF. A new drawing's export area is US Letter upright, 8.5 × 11 inches (1700 × 2200 of the drawing's pixels, which measure 200 to the inch). Its width and height are shown and typed in **Pixels**, **Imperial** (inches) or **Metric** (millimeters), chosen under Units; drag its edge on the canvas to move it. In inches and millimeters, **Pixels per inch** under Output sets how much detail a PNG or JPG gets, and its label says what that comes to (2550 × 3300 px for Letter at 300); the page and the drawing stay as they are. In pixels, Resolution's 1×, 2× and so on does that instead. Drawings saved before open in pixels and keep their size. The area's display settings (Show, Shade outside, Clip guides, Lock aspect) share one row, as do the parts of the drawing to include Choose the format under Output in the Export area section; the Export button on the toolbar follows it:
  - **JPG** has an adjustable quality, and its background is always white, since a JPG can't be see-through
  - **PDF** keeps every ray, guide, face and label as shapes rather than pixels, so it stays sharp at any size. Its page is the export area at its print size, the inches or millimeters it shows (8.5 × 11 inches for a new drawing), so it needs no resolution; hairlines, dashes and labels are sized for the page as a PNG's are for its pixels. The VP labels are set in Courier, which every PDF reader has
- **Scale the drawing** to fit the export area. Drag the round handle just off the export area's bottom-right corner in toward the middle to shrink everything (the horizon, vanishing points, rays, guides and boxes), or out to enlarge it. The export area itself and line weights stay as they are. Every mode scales together, so switching perspective afterwards gives the same boxes as scaling after the switch. The lens or panorama in use stops the drawing at the smallest or largest size it can take. - Longer explanations in the panel sit behind an **ⓘ Info** button (and the introduction behind **ⓘ About**, beside the title): hover over it to read the paragraph in a pop-up, which stays while the pointer is on the button or the pop-up. So does every other paragraph: what the drawing is doing at the moment, like 3-point's camera check, a lens's measurements, rays spaced along a box or how columns and rows run, sits behind an Info button labeled with what it's about (**ⓘ Camera check**, **ⓘ Lens measurements**, **ⓘ Rays spaced along B1** and so on), and the empty box list shows **ⓘ No boxes yet**
- Number boxes accept values past the end of each slider, and the slider stretches to fit; releasing a slider at its end doubles its range
- Undo from the toolbar or with Ctrl/⌘ Z; redo with Ctrl/⌘ Shift Z or Ctrl/⌘ Y. The shortcuts also work right after you use a slider, checkbox, color picker or dropdown. Only while you're typing in a number or text box are they left to that box
- Save a setup as a JSON file and load it back later. A file that isn't a setup is refused. In one that is, values that don't make sense fall back to the defaults, and boxes with unusable corners are left out

Everything runs in a single `index.html` with no build step. Work is saved in the visitor's own browser.
